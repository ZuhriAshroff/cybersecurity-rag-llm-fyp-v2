Title: Compliance Frameworks — PCI DSS, GDPR, ISO 27001, and NIST CSF
Source: PCI Security Standards Council / European Commission GDPR / ISO/IEC 27001:2022 / NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0

Cybersecurity compliance frameworks provide structured requirements and guidelines that help organisations protect data, demonstrate security posture to stakeholders, and meet regulatory obligations. Understanding major frameworks is essential for security professionals working in regulated industries.

PCI DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard)
PCI DSS governs protection of payment card data. Version 4.0 (2022) contains 12 requirements across six goals, applicable to any organisation that stores, processes, or transmits cardholder data (CHD).

Key requirements include: installing and maintaining network security controls (firewalls, segment the CDE); protecting stored account data (no CVV storage, truncated or tokenised PAN); protecting cardholder data in transit with strong cryptography (TLS 1.2+); using and updating anti-malware; developing and maintaining secure systems (patch management, SSDLC); restricting access to system components by business need-to-know; identifying users and authenticating access to system components (MFA required for all access to the CDE); restricting physical access; logging and monitoring all access to network resources and cardholder data; testing security systems regularly (penetration testing, ASV scans); maintaining an information security policy.

Annual assessment by a Qualified Security Assessor (QSA) is required for the largest merchants; smaller merchants may complete a Self-Assessment Questionnaire (SAQ).

GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)
The EU's GDPR, effective May 2018, governs the processing of personal data of EU residents. Key principles: lawfulness, fairness, transparency; purpose limitation; data minimisation; accuracy; storage limitation; integrity and confidentiality (security); and accountability.

Security obligations (Article 32): Implement appropriate technical and organisational measures — pseudonymisation and encryption, ability to ensure confidentiality and integrity, ability to restore availability after incidents, and regular testing of security measures.

Breach notification: Notify the supervisory authority within 72 hours of awareness of a personal data breach likely to result in risk to individuals. Notify affected individuals without undue delay when the breach is high risk.

Penalties: Up to €20M or 4% of global annual turnover (whichever is higher) for serious violations.

ISO/IEC 27001:2022
ISO 27001 is the international standard for Information Security Management Systems (ISMS). Certification requires implementation of a structured ISMS encompassing risk assessment, security controls from Annex A (93 controls across 4 themes in the 2022 version), and a Plan-Do-Check-Act improvement cycle.

ISO 27001 is principle-based rather than prescriptive — organisations choose and implement controls appropriate to their risk profile. Certification is awarded by accredited certification bodies following third-party audit. Widely accepted as evidence of security maturity by enterprise customers.

NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 (CSF 2.0)
NIST CSF provides a voluntary, risk-based framework for managing cybersecurity risk. CSF 2.0 (2024) has six functions: Govern (new in 2.0), Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover. Each function contains categories and subcategories mapped to security activities.

CSF is widely adopted across US critical infrastructure, used by organisations globally as a risk management tool, and referenced in regulatory guidance. Unlike PCI DSS or ISO 27001, CSF is not prescriptive and does not have a certification mechanism.

Framework Selection
Large organisations often operate under multiple frameworks simultaneously — PCI DSS for payment card processing, GDPR/CCPA for data privacy, ISO 27001 for overall ISMS, and NIST CSF for risk management. Crosswalks (mapping tables between frameworks) help identify overlapping controls, reducing duplication of effort.
